# Verified facts (checked 2026-11-21)

1. Language models first learn through "pretraining", a process of predicting the next word in huge amounts of text. Source: OpenAI, "Why language models hallucinate" (openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate), section "How hallucinations originate from next-word prediction".
2. Hallucinations are "plausible but false statements generated by language models"; the model can confidently generate an answer that isn't true. Same OpenAI page (its example: a chatbot confidently gave three different, wrong titles for an author's PhD dissertation).
3. Anthropic's docs say even the most advanced models, such as Claude, "can sometimes generate text that is factually incorrect", known as hallucination. Source: platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations.
4. The script's "one small piece at a time" is a plain-English simplification of generating text token by token; show it as words appearing one after another, no claim about "tokens" or numbers.
